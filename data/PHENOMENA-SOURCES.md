# Observed objects, illustrative close-ups

These seven real objects are destinations in the main atlas, sharing its canvas, free camera, animated travel and text/ANSI export. `/phenomena` and `/blackhole` redirect into that atlas. Near each destination, a local model evaluates compact analytic surfaces, authored emission fields or compiled geodesic tables directly at character-cell rays. The close-ups are interpretations, not measured three-dimensional reconstructions, live feeds or forecasts.

Six directions come from a cached [CDS/SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr/simbad/) query, retaining the underlying coordinate references. Betelgeuse uses its exact existing HYG v4.2 position and catalog index. Rounded public distances are adopted where indicated below; the precision of a direction does not imply equally precise distance. Catalog positions remain fixed during travel.

The six appearance studies use normalized model units, explicitly distinguished from measured body radii in the interface and metadata. Their detailed geometry, relative dimensions, motion rates and colors are illustrative. Throughout travel, the atlas combines measured HYG objects with **60,000 finite 3D Milky Way light samples**: 32,000 disk samples, 16,000 central-concentration samples and 12,000 nuclear samples at radii of 0.1–10 pc. Each has a fixed position relative to Sagittarius A*, an authored light weight and a color. Translation therefore changes direction through parallax and brightness through distance. These are statistically authored samples, not individually measured stars, a calibrated stellar census or a physical dynamical simulation.

The native sampler builds an observer-dependent index of the finite sources and their inverse-square light proxies, with finite near-source softening and display clipping. Faint positive source contributions are retained and summed before display thresholding. An artistic overview exposure then rises smoothly from 1× in the interior to at most 32× outside the disk, revealing unresolved populations without changing their stored positions or light weights. Its ellipsoidal transition is defined in Galactic coordinates and is a display policy, not a measured exposure or Galactic density law. The Solar neighborhood and inner views retain 1×; the result is not calibrated photometry.

Near Sagittarius A*, that same source index is queried in the compiled geodesics' escaping outgoing directions. **This lenses the authored spatial context, not measured HYG stars.** Captured rays remain black unless foreground disk emission is visible. The outgoing-direction query is a far-field approximation: the nearest authored nuclear sources lie at least 0.1 pc from the hole, compared with roughly 0.000062 pc for the outer 150-Schwarzschild-radius camera domain. It does not solve a finite-source geodesic boundary problem for every sample. The compiled observer domain is 6–150 Schwarzschild radii; the gradual 150–50-radius transition is a display choice. Kerr spin is absent.

The source-coordinate basis follows the [ERFA/SOFA ICRS-to-Galactic rotation](https://github.com/liberfa/erfa/blob/master/src/icrs2g.c), which implements the Hipparcos convention using the canonical north-pole direction and ascending-node angle. [ESO's Milky Way overview](https://supernova.eso.org/exhibition/1004/?lang=en) supports the broad disk, spiral structure and central concentration. The six authored streams and their precise geometry are not an observational fit. `compile_galaxy.py` produces `artifacts/galaxy-stars.bin`: 1.92 MB of position, light-proxy, color and group records, about 0.982 MB compressed. `artifacts/galaxy-stars.json` records its basis, center, dimensions, seed and hash. The production context consists of these spatial points, without a painted galaxy panorama or additive Gaussian haze. Decorative samples have no measured proper motions or selectable catalog identities.

`compile_destinations.py` writes `artifacts/phenomena-destinations.json`, including the coordinate query, source hashes, adopted distances, physical quantities and model scales. Raw coordinates and curated source facts are cached in `data/sources/phenomena-simbad-coordinates.json` and `data/sources/phenomena-anchor-facts.json`.

`compile_phenomena.py` bakes the two authored volumetric fields deterministically. `artifacts/phenomena.json` records the format, seed and asset hash. Neither field is derived from telescope imagery. The black-hole lookup tables are built separately by `compile_lensing.py`; their presence does not make the illustrative disk or accretion state a reconstruction of Sagittarius A* observations.

The arrival gradually interpolates straight and deflected ray directions from 150 to 50 Schwarzschild radii for a continuous reveal. This visual transition is not a newly integrated weak-field solution; full-strength views use the compiled Schwarzschild transfer.
